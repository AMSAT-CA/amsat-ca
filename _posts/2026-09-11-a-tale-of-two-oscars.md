This is a tale of two OSCARs (Orbiting Satellite Carrying Amateur Radio), American OSCAR I and Canada-OSCAR 128.

### American OSCAR I

In 1961, when I was at Case Western Reserve University, a group of amateur radio operators combined their effort to listen to a satellite. One student's task was the orbital calculations. One student's task was the azimuth control. One student's task the elevation control. One student's task was the frequency control. I had the good fortune to hear the distinctive HI HI in Morse code of the American OSCAR I.

73, John DeDourek, VE9TFB, ex K8TFB

### Canada-OSCAR 128

In 2026, when I was in Prince Edward Island, I used my equipment to listen to a satellite. My cell phone could track azimuth and elevation. My portable radio was a Yaesu VX-8GR set to receive amplitude modulation, and the antenna was an Arrow II 146/440-14, with a 4-element VHF Yagi. I had the good fortune to hear CQ DE VA7UVS in Morse code, the call sign of Canada-OSCAR 128, CO-128, transmitting on 145.875 MHz. An image of the equipment is shown.

73, Brent Petersen, VY2/VE9EX, W1IY
